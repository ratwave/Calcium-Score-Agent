# Living with a High Calcium Score (CAC > 600): Baseline Guidance

**Audience:** adults with a very high CAC score discussing prevention with their clinician.

> This guide is educational and not medical advice. A CAC score above 600 usually indicates high atherosclerotic burden and should be managed with a clinician-led prevention plan.

## What to avoid (food, habits, and care gaps)

1. **Smoking or vaping nicotine**
   - Strongly linked to progression of coronary disease and major cardiovascular events.

2. **Stopping preventive medications without clinician guidance**
   - Abruptly stopping statins, blood pressure drugs, or diabetes medications can increase risk.

3. **Diet patterns high in:**
   - processed meats
   - trans fats
   - frequent ultra-processed foods
   - excess refined carbohydrates / sugar-sweetened drinks
   - high sodium intake (especially with hypertension or heart failure risk)

4. **Sedentary lifestyle**
   - Prolonged inactivity is associated with worse cardiometabolic risk.

5. **Uncontrolled risk factors**
   - untreated high LDL-C
   - uncontrolled blood pressure
   - uncontrolled diabetes/prediabetes
   - untreated sleep apnea

6. **Heavy alcohol use**
   - Raises blood pressure, arrhythmia risk, and cardiometabolic burden.

7. **Ignoring symptoms**
   - New chest pressure, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or neurologic symptoms need prompt clinical evaluation.

## What to prioritize eating

1. **Mediterranean-style pattern (best evidence base)**
   - Vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts/seeds
   - Extra-virgin olive oil as primary fat
   - Fish (especially oily fish) regularly
   - Limited red/processed meat and sweets
   - PREDIMED and related evidence support reduced major cardiovascular events in high-risk adults.

2. **Fiber-forward choices**
   - Oats, beans/lentils, vegetables, berries, nuts.
   - Helps LDL reduction and glycemic control.

3. **Protein quality**
   - Prefer fish, legumes, and lean proteins over processed or fatty red meats.

4. **Sodium awareness**
   - Especially important if blood pressure is elevated.

## What to prioritize doing

1. **Aggressive risk-factor control with your clinician**
   - LDL-C lowering is central (often statin-first, then add-on therapies when needed).
   - Blood pressure, glucose, body weight, and sleep quality matter in parallel.

2. **Physical activity routine**
   - At least 150 minutes/week moderate aerobic activity (or equivalent),
   - plus resistance training 2+ days/week if appropriate.

3. **Medication adherence**
   - Consistency is key for long-horizon event reduction.

4. **Sleep and stress management**
   - Aim for consistent sleep and practical stress reduction techniques.

5. **Regular follow-up and shared decision-making**
   - Review lipids, blood pressure, glycemia, kidney function, side effects, and overall prevention targets.

## Clarifying expectations about plaque "removal"

- Current therapies primarily reduce events by stabilizing plaque and lowering atherogenic burden.
- Some studies show **modest plaque regression** in coronary imaging with very intensive lipid lowering.
- **CAC score itself often does not decrease** even when risk is better controlled; calcification can progress while plaque stability and outcomes improve.

## Active and emerging research areas to watch

1. **Intensive LDL-C lowering and plaque regression**
   - Statins + ezetimibe + PCSK9-pathway therapy in selected patients.
   - Imaging studies (e.g., IVUS/CCTA) show changes in plaque burden/composition.

2. **Lipoprotein(a) lowering**
   - Antisense/siRNA programs (e.g., pelacarsen, olpasiran) are in large outcomes trials.

3. **Inflammation-targeted prevention**
   - Low-dose colchicine has outcome data in chronic coronary disease populations.
   - Additional anti-inflammatory pathways remain under study.

4. **Next-generation lipid biology**
   - CETP-related and RNA-based agents are being evaluated for residual risk.

5. **Plaque biology and local therapies**
   - Early-stage work includes targeted delivery and plaque-specific imaging/biology, but no routine clinical "plaque removal" therapy is available yet.

## Key references (high-level)

- 2023 AHA/ACC Multisociety Guideline for Chronic Coronary Disease.
- PREDIMED (Mediterranean diet and CV event reduction).
- GLAGOV and related imaging studies of intensive LDL lowering.
- Ongoing Lp(a) outcomes programs (e.g., Lp(a)HORIZON, OCEAN(a)-Outcomes).
